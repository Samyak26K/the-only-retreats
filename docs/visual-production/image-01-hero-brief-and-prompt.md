## 1. Creative Direction

This is not a product shot.

This is the founding still of The Only Retreats.

The first frame must do what Aman does at arrival, what Hermès does with an object, what National Geographic does with place, and what Apple does with silence: **one truth, perfectly held**.

The jar is not selling itself.  
The jar is a vessel that has survived a journey from Kullu’s high villages — Pulag, Rumsu, Hallan — and now rests, briefly, on stone that could have come from the same mountains.

**Emotional contract with the visitor**

Within one second they must feel:

- Ancient India (without costume theatre)
- The Himalayas (specific, high, thin-aired — not generic Alps)
- Silence
- Luxury (through restraint, never ornament)
- Craftsmanship (suggested by wood, brass, bilona-adjacent quiet)
- Authenticity (mist, stone, real light)
- Living heritage (a place that still breathes)

**What this image must never be**

- Ecommerce white seamless
- Fake CGI bottle render
- Over-propped “flat lay lifestyle”
- Hyper-saturated turmeric Instagram food
- Temple cliché / religious appropriation spectacle
- Aggressive gold foil luxury porn

**Brand line locked on label**

```text
The Only Retreats
Himalayan Badri Cow Bilona Ghee
The Forgotten Path
```

## 2. Composition

**Aspect:** Landscape 16:9 (desktop hero primary). Leave a companion crop mindset for 4:5 later — but generate the master as 16:9.

**Structure (rule of thirds + Apple negative space)**

- **Foreground (lower third):** Aged Himalayan stone ledge, tactile, uneven, honest. The bottle stands naturally — not floating, not perfectly centred like a catalogue.
- **Subject (lower-right to centre-right third):** The exact Only Retreats bottle, three-quarter to near-front angle so the label reads clearly. Glass catches a single soft specular; ghee within reads as warm butter-gold, never neon.
- **Mid-ground (sparse):** One or two restrained heritage accents only — a fragment of ancient wood, a quiet brass utensil partially in soft focus, a whisper of wild Himalayan herbs. These must feel found, not arranged by a prop stylist.
- **Background (upper two-thirds):** Kullu Valley at golden hour — layered ridges, soft morning mist in the valleys, warm sunlight raking distant slopes. Landscape is co-protagonist.
- **Negative space:** Open air and mist on the left and upper frame for future typography overlay (product name / shloka). Do not fill every corner.

**Hierarchy**

1. Atmosphere / valley
2. Bottle as artifact
3. Stone and material honesty
4. Whisper of craft props

If the eye goes to props first, the frame fails.

---

## 3. Camera

- Medium-format / Leica documentary still aesthetic
- Camera position: **slightly low three-quarter**, as if the viewer has just set the jar down on a stone wall overlooking the valley
- Height: just below label centre so the bottle feels grounded and dignified
- Distance: environmental product — bottle large enough to read, landscape still sovereign
- No Dutch tilt. No drone theatricality. No fisheye.

**Feeling:** A quiet photographer who arrived at dawn and waited.

---

## 4. Lens

- **Primary recommendation:** 50mm full-frame equivalent (natural human perspective, Hermès object in place)
- Acceptable alternate: 35mm if more valley is needed — never wider than ~28mm (distortion kills luxury)
- Aperture feeling: **f/2.8–f/4** — bottle sharp through label; background softly separated but still legible as Kullu, not creamy abstract bokeh balls
- Micro-contrast: Leica-like; edges honest, not HDR-crushed

---

## 5. Lighting

**Key:** Soft Himalayan golden-hour sunlight from camera-left / slightly behind, warm and low.

**Fill:** Gentle open-sky bounce from mist — no studio softbox look.

**Rim:** A thin natural rim on the glass shoulder from the valley light; restrained.

**Atmosphere:** Morning mist in the valley folds — luminous, not fog-machine dense.

**Shadows:** Soft, long, believable on stone. No black crushed holes. No beauty-dish butterfly lighting on the bottle.

**Specular on glass:** One primary highlight; secondary whispers only. Avoid chrome-car-advert reflection maps.

## 9. Production-Ready AI Image Prompt

_(700–1200 words · GPT Images / Flux Kontext Pro / Midjourney V7 / Ideogram)_

```text
Create a single defining hero photograph for The Only Retreats, an origin-first luxury heritage house — not an ecommerce brand. This image is the first thing a visitor sees on the Himalayan Badri Cow Bilona Ghee product page and must become the emblematic still of the entire brand. It should feel as if it were photographed for a quiet collaboration between Aman Resorts, Hermès, Apple, Leica, and National Geographic: museum quality, emotionally restrained, timeless, and absolutely real. No fake CGI sheen. No stock-photo tourism. No aggressive luxury kitsch. Luxury only through silence, materials, and light.

SUBJECT AND PRODUCT ACCURACY
Photograph the exact The Only Retreats glass bottle of Himalayan Badri Cow Bilona Ghee standing naturally on aged Himalayan stone. The bottle must look like a physical object with weight, thickness of glass, subtle imperfections, and believable refraction — never a 3D product visualization. Inside the bottle, the ghee reads as natural butter-gold and warm cream-gold, the colour of slow bilona-made clarified fat from Badri cow milk — never neon yellow, never neon orange, never plastic. The label must be clearly legible and elegantly typeset on a warm cream or soft off-white ground with refined charcoal typography, and it must read exactly, in hierarchical lines:

The Only Retreats
Himalayan Badri Cow Bilona Ghee
The Forgotten Path

Do not invent additional slogan lines. Do not add QR codes, barcodes, nutritional panels, or fake certification stickers. The label should feel letterpress-adjacent, quiet, and artisanal — Hermès object labelling, not supermarket Ayurveda branding.

SETTING AND PLACE
Place the bottle on a ledge or low wall of aged Himalayan stone: rough, mineral, cool-grey and brown, worn by weather, with honest pores and soft moss hints in the crevices. Behind and beyond the stone, open into the Kullu Valley of Himachal Pradesh at approximately dawn-to-golden-hour: layered mountain ridges, high-altitude clarity, terraces and forested slopes suggested in soft distance, the living geography around villages such as Pulag, Rumsu, and Hallan without needing readable signage. Soft Himalayan morning mist pools in the valley folds, luminous and delicate, catching warm sunlight. The landscape must feel specific to the Indian Himalaya — cedar-dark greens, stone villages as tiny texture if visible at all, thin air — not generic Swiss Alps, not fantasy peaks.

SUPPORTING ELEMENTS — EXTREME RESTRAINT
Around the bottle, allow only a whisper of living heritage, never a prop explosion. Include at most: a fragment of ancient wooden texture (a worn timber edge or bilona-adjacent wood plane partially in frame), one subtle antique brass utensil (a ladle bowl or small vessel rim) softly out of focus, and a sparse touch of wild Himalayan herbs — dusty, wild, desaturated greens, as if wind-placed, not florist-arranged. These elements should occupy the periphery and mid-ground blur so the eye still goes first to atmosphere and then to the bottle. If any prop competes with the bottle or the valley, remove it. Empty stone and mist are more luxurious than clutter.

COMPOSITION
Landscape orientation, cinematic 16:9 master frame. Compose with disciplined negative space. Position the bottle in the lower-right to centre-right third, grounded on the stone, standing upright and natural — not centred like a catalogue hero, not floating. Keep the left and upper portions more open: mist, ridge lines, and quiet sky for the emotional field and potential typography overlay. Use a slight low three-quarter camera angle, as if the viewer has just placed the jar on a stone wall overlooking the valley and paused. The bottle should be large enough for the full label to read, yet small enough that the Himalayas remain sovereign. Foreground stone texture tactile and sharp; mid-ground props soft; background valley layered and atmospheric. One primary subject hierarchy only. No Dutch angle. No fisheye. No drone vertigo.

CAMERA AND LENS LANGUAGE
Shoot with the character of a Leica or medium-format documentary still: intimate, precise, unsentimental. Prefer a 50mm full-frame-equivalent perspective for natural human seeing; a restrained 35mm is acceptable if it expands valley presence without distorting the bottle. Aperture feeling around f/2.8 to f/4: the bottle and label critically sharp, the valley beautifully separated yet still readable as place. Micro-contrast and tonal honesty over HDR crunch. Subtle fine film grain is welcome; plastic skin-smoothing and AI gloss are forbidden.

LIGHTING
Natural warm Himalayan golden-hour sunlight as the key, coming from camera-left and slightly behind, low and gentle. Soft morning mist acts as a giant natural diffuser in the distance. On the glass, allow one clean primary specular highlight on the shoulder and a restrained secondary whisper — never a studio reflection map, never chrome-car advertising glare. Shadows on stone should be soft, long, and believable. Avoid beauty-dish butterfly lighting, ring-light catchlights, and black crushed studio voids. The light should feel like it arrived after a cold night in Kullu, not after a softbox kit was unpacked.

COLOUR AND GRADE
Unified palette: warm sand and apricot golden-hour light; morning mist in blue-grey veils; forest moss and charcoal ridges; aged stone greys; antique muted brass; wild dusty herb greens; natural butter-gold ghee; cream label with charcoal type. Overall grade slightly desaturated, cinematic, editorial — Kinfolk and Cereal Magazine restraint meets National Geographic place truth. No teal-and-orange YouTube grade. No hyper-saturated travel poster skies. No magenta haze. White balance warm but adult.

MOOD AND BRAND EMOTION
The image must immediately communicate: Ancient India; the Himalayas; silence; luxury; craftsmanship; authenticity; living heritage. The emotional temperature is quiet awe — Aman arrival silence — not excitement. The bottle is an artifact of The Forgotten Path: a jar that carries a valley. The viewer should feel they have not opened a shop; they have stepped into an origin.

STRICT NEGATIVES
Do not create fake CGI, 3D renders, octane-like product shots, or unreal glass. Do not use pure white seamless ecommerce backdrops. Do not add people, faces, hands, tourists, temples, prayer flags as decoration, deities, or religious spectacle. Do not add price tags, shopping bags, or modern packaging waste. Do not overcrowd with spices, chili piles, turmeric hills, excessive copper pots, or “Ayurveda flat lay” clichés. Do not use marble slabs, acrylic risers, velvet, or gold-foil luxury porn. Do not invent illegible garbage text or misspell the label. Do not make the ghee neon. Do not make the mountains generic wallpaper.

FINISH
Photoreal, museum-quality still photography. Timeless. Emotional. Authentic. One frame worthy of being the defining image of The Only Retreats. Single image, no collage, no split screen, no watermark, no border, no caption burned in.
```
